# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

UALC exists because no single person can own every appliance. It only works if
people who own hardware we never will feel able to show up, report what they
saw, and sometimes be wrong in public. We are committed to making that
possible for everyone, regardless of experience level, background, identity or
how fluent their English is.

## Expected behaviour

* **Be specific rather than dismissive.** "This frame decodes differently on
  my unit, here is the capture" is the whole point of this project.
* **Treat a negative result as a contribution.** "I tried this and my
  appliance refused" is real information, and often more useful than a
  success. Nobody should feel they have to produce a working result to be
  worth listening to.
* **Assume good faith about hardware reports you cannot reproduce.** You
  usually cannot check someone else's appliance. Ask for detail; do not imply
  dishonesty.
* **Be patient with protocol work.** Reverse-engineering is slow and partly
  guesswork. Wrong hypotheses are a normal part of it.
* **Accept correction without defensiveness**, and offer it without contempt.
* **Respect the honesty rules** as engineering discipline rather than
  bureaucracy. Asking someone to downgrade an overstated support claim is not
  an insult, and being asked is not a judgement of your competence.

## Unacceptable behaviour

* Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments; sustained disruption.
* Discrimination or personal attacks of any kind.
* Publishing someone's private information — including information
  accidentally leaked in an unsanitized capture. **If you spot personal data
  in a contribution, report it privately; do not quote it.**
* Pressuring anyone to share manufacturer account credentials, run a risky
  experiment on an appliance they depend on, or test something they are not
  comfortable testing. **Nobody owes this project a hardware test.**
* Deliberately overstating verification status, or pressuring a maintainer to
  accept an unverifiable claim.
* Submitting code intended to compromise a user's host, or knowingly
  contributing code whose licence does not permit it.

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, commit messages,
discussions, and any private correspondence conducted on the project's behalf.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the maintainers via the contact in
[`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), marking the subject "Conduct". Reports are
handled confidentially. If a report concerns a maintainer, you may ask that
they be excluded from handling it.

Security vulnerabilities follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) instead — please
do not open a public issue first.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying standards and will take action they
judge appropriate and proportionate — from a private word, through editing or
removing a contribution, to a temporary or permanent ban. Maintainers are held
to the same standards as everybody else.

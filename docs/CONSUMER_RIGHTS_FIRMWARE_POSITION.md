# "We own our devices, we decide when they update" — supporting context

**Status: project documentation, no code/architecture change.** This captures supporting
legal/policy context for the project's existing ownership position — it is framed as context,
not as a legal conclusion. The underlying legal research itself is not repeated here; see the
link below for the verified citations and their source.

## The position (unchanged, already stated elsewhere)

We own our devices. A device that works correctly and meets our needs should not be changed by
its manufacturer without our approval. Firmware updates, including security updates, should be
evaluated and installed only with informed owner consent.

## Supporting context — linked, not duplicated

The full legal research backing this position (EU Directives 2019/771 and 2019/770, the Danish
implementation, Vietnam's Law No. 19/2023/QH15, verified complaint channels for all three
jurisdictions, and a sample complaint letter) is published and kept current at
**https://holisticdata.com/device-ownership.html**. This note intentionally does not restate
those citations — maintaining the same legal text in two places risks one copy drifting out of
date while looking equally authoritative. Read the live page for the actual provisions and their
current verification date.

**Corrected 2026-11-24**: an earlier version of this note cited Vietnam "Article 25" for
quality-assurance obligations, unverified against the primary text at the time. The live
Holistic Data page has the correct, verified citation (Article 30, warranty obligations,
cross-confirmed two independent ways) — this note no longer repeats the superseded citation.

## Explicit framing, restated

This is supporting context for a stated position and ownership philosophy, not a legal opinion,
not a claim of non-compliance by any named company, and not a representation that automatic
firmware updates are unconditionally prohibited anywhere. If this is ever used publicly or in any
formal context, it should first be reviewed against the actual primary legislative text and,
ideally, by someone qualified to give legal advice in the relevant jurisdiction.
